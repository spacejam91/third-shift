What it handles

The paperwork with an expiry date

Most of it lives in an inbox, a filing cabinet and a calendar reminder that’s easy to miss.

  • Subcontractor clearances

    WSIB clearance certificates on file before a subcontractor starts, renewed before they lapse, with copies kept. Clearances expire, so the dates matter.

  • Insurance certificates

    Certificates of insurance from subcontractors and suppliers kept with the job, the end dates watched, and new ones asked for before the old ones run out.

  • Training and tickets

    Working at heights, first aid, WHMIS, forklift and the rest, tracked by person, with a list of who’s due before anyone’s card runs out.

  • Licences and registrations

    Trade licences, professional registrations and permits renewed on time, with the reminder going to whoever does the renewing.

  • Ontario filings and policies

    The filings and written policies that come with growing, like the accessibility compliance report at 20 employees or the electronic monitoring policy at 25, on the calendar with what each one needs.

  • Customer questionnaires

    Security and supplier questionnaires from bigger customers, and insurance renewal forms, drafted from your own records. A person checks and approves every answer before it goes back.

How it works

Nothing lapses because nobody was watching

It keeps the records and the dates. You sign what gets signed.

  1. 1

    Gathered

    What you already have, in a shared drive, an inbox or a filing cabinet, goes into one list with each document’s owner and expiry date.

  2. 2

    Checked

    Every night the list is checked against the dates, and against who starts work or is due for training soon.

  3. 3

    Chased

    Reminders go out ahead of time to whoever needs to renew, in words you approved, and missing documents are asked for.

  4. 4

    Filed

    New certificates are filed where they belong and the list is updated.

  5. 5

    Summarized

    The morning email says what’s missing, what’s due soon and what needs your signature.

What stays with a person

The records are kept for you. The sign-off is yours.

  • Filings and signatures

    Anything that gets filed or signed, like a compliance report or a written policy, is prepared for you to check. A person in your business signs it.

  • Legal questions

    We keep records and dates, not legal opinions. Where a rule needs interpreting, your lawyer, accountant or safety advisor makes the call.

  • Staff records

    Training and registration records stay in your own systems, read-only wherever possible, and only the people you choose can see them.

  • Every run logged

    A plain-English record of what it checked, filed and chased, so you can show what was done and when.

Questions

What owners ask

Will this make us compliant?

No tool can promise that, and we don’t give legal advice. It keeps the records, watches the dates and chases what’s missing, so the work compliance depends on doesn’t slip. What the rules require of your business is for you and your advisors to confirm.

Where are the records kept?

In your own systems: the shared drive, inbox and spreadsheets you already use. Access is read-only wherever possible, and you can switch it off at any time.

We only have a few subcontractors. Is it worth it?

Maybe not on its own. It’s often added to another build, like supplier invoices or the weekly numbers. We’ll tell you plainly on the call whether it’s worth it for you.

What does a build cost?

Every build has a fixed price, written on the one-page plan before any work starts, along with what it costs to run each month. Any software or AI accounts it uses are in your name and billed to you directly. You know both numbers before you commit.
