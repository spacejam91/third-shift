What it handles

Out of one head and onto a schedule

If it happens on a schedule and follows the same steps, it can usually run without anyone remembering to start it.

  • Written down first

    We sit with the person who does it and write down every step, including the ones they do without thinking.

  • Run on a schedule

    Daily, weekly, at month-end, or when something happens, like a new order or a form coming in.

  • A log of every run

    What it did, what it touched and what it held back, in plain English.

  • Safe when things change

    If a file moves or a column is renamed, it stops and tells someone instead of carrying on wrong.

  • Holidays and sick days

    The routine runs whether or not the person who used to do it is in that day.

  • Easy to hand over

    New staff read the log and the written steps instead of learning by looking over someone’s shoulder.

How it works

Watched once, written down, then built

Steps that need judgment stay with a person. The rest runs on its own.

  1. 1

    Shadowed

    We watch the routine done once, start to finish, and write down every step.

  2. 2

    Agreed

    You check the written version and mark the steps that need a decision.

  3. 3

    Built

    The rest is built to run on its schedule, in the systems you already use.

  4. 4

    Run side by side

    It runs alongside the person who does it today until the results match.

  5. 5

    Handed over

    With monitoring, the log of every run, and the written steps.

What stays with a person

Decisions stay with people

  • Judgment calls

    Anything that needs a decision is prepared and handed to a person, not decided by the automation.

  • Money

    No money is paid or sent without a person signing off, unless you decide otherwise.

  • Its own login

    Each automation has its own access, so you can switch it off without touching anything else.

  • Stops when unsure

    When something fails or looks wrong, it stops and tells a person instead of guessing.

Questions

What owners ask

What if the process isn’t written down anywhere?

That’s the usual case. Writing it down is the first step of every build like this.

What if the person who does it is leaving?

Then it’s worth writing down now, while they can still walk us through it.

Can we change it later?

Yes. When the routine changes, the automation can be changed to match, and the written steps with it.

How long does a first build take?

First builds typically ship within a few weeks. We run it alongside your team until it’s trusted, then hand it over.
