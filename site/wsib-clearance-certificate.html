What it is

Proof your subcontractor is covered

A clearance is a number WSIB gives a registered business that is up to date on its premiums and reporting. Only businesses in good standing can get one.

  • Why you check it

    When you hire a subcontractor, you’re the principal for that work. While their clearance is valid, WSIB won’t hold you liable for premiums and other amounts the subcontractor owes it. Without one, you may be liable for what they owe, up to the labour portion of your contract with them.

  • In construction, it’s required

    A clearance has to be in place before any construction work begins and for the whole job. Working without a valid one is an offence for the contractor and for whoever hired them, with fines of up to $100,000.

  • How long it lasts

    Up to 90 days from the date it’s issued. Most expire on the 20th of February, May, August and November, and WSIB issues the next one automatically if the account stays in good standing.

  • Long jobs

    The clearance has to cover the full length of the job, so if the work runs past the expiry date, you need the next one.

How to check one

Look it up, save it, check it again

Looking up a clearance is free, and WSIB’s quick access service needs no login.

  1. 1

    Open quick access clearances

    On wsib.ca, quick access clearances lets you find, view and download a clearance without an account.

  2. 2

    Search for the business

    Search by business name, phone number, CRA business name, address, city or postal code.

  3. 3

    Save it with the job

    Download the clearance and keep it on file. Principals and contractors have to keep clearance records for at least three years.

  4. 4

    Check again before it runs out

    The next clearance isn’t available until after the renewal date. With a WSIB online services account, you can keep a list of your contractors and get reminder emails.

Questions

What owners ask

Do I need one for a home renovation?

WSIB says clearances aren’t required for contractors hired by a home’s resident, occupant or a family member for home repairs or renovations. A business hiring subcontractors is a different case.

Does it cost anything?

Looking one up is free, and so are clearances. A newly registered business that needs one at registration usually pays a $250 advance, which WSIB credits toward its future premiums.

What if a subcontractor can’t get one?

Only businesses that are registered and up to date with WSIB can get a clearance. If their account is marked ineligible, they have to bring it up to date first. Whether they can work for you in the meantime is a question for WSIB or your advisor.
